# PCOS Exercise and Mental Health Guide

## Why Exercise Matters in PCOS

Exercise improves insulin sensitivity independent of weight loss. Even without weight change, regular exercise reduces androgen levels, improves ovulation rates, and boosts mood. Exercise is medicine for PCOS.

## Aerobic Exercise

**Goal**: 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity weekly (about 30 min, 5 days/week).

Moderate intensity = you can talk but not sing while exercising.

### Examples
- Brisk walking (10,000+ steps daily is a great starting goal)
- Cycling
- Swimming
- Dancing
- Jogging
- Rowing

## Resistance Training

**Goal**: 2-3 sessions per week, 20-40 minutes each.

Resistance training builds muscle, which acts as a glucose sink, improving insulin resistance substantially.

### Examples
- Bodyweight exercises (squats, lunges, push-ups, planks)
- Dumbbells / kettlebells
- Resistance bands
- Gym machines

**Hint**: A simple at-home circuit — 3 rounds of 10 squats, 10 push-ups (or modified), 30-second plank, 10 lunges per leg — is effective.

## Combining Both

| Day | Type | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Aerobic (walk/cycle) | 30 min |
| Tue | Resistance training | 30 min |
| Wed | Aerobic | 30 min |
| Thu | Resistance training | 30 min |
| Fri | Aerobic | 30 min |
| Sat | Gentle movement (yoga, stretching) | 30-60 min |
| Sun | Rest / light walk | — |

## Starting Tips

1. **Start small**: Begin with 10-15 minutes and build up — consistency beats intensity
2. **Find joy**: Pick activities you actually like so you'll stick with them
3. **Add NEAT**: Non-exercise activity thermogenesis — take stairs, park farther, walk during phone calls
4. **Track steps**: Aim to gradually increase daily steps toward 10,000
5. **Listen to your body**: Rest when needed; overtraining adds stress and raises cortisol

## The Stress Connection

Stress raises cortisol, which worsens insulin resistance and hormonal imbalance. Managing stress is a legitimate part of PCOS treatment.

### Stress Reduction Techniques
- **Deep breathing**: 4-4-4 breathing (inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 4s) signals calm to the nervous system
- **Meditation**: 5-10 minutes daily using apps or guided audio
- **Journaling**: Write down worries to reduce mental load
- **Nature time**: 20 minutes outdoors daily lowers cortisol
- **Yoga**: Combines movement, breathing, and mindfulness

## Mental Health in PCOS

Women with PCOS face significantly higher rates of:
- **Depression** (roughly 2x higher)
- **Anxiety** (roughly 3x higher)
- **Eating disorders** (bulimia and binge eating in particular)
- **Body image distress** (acne, hirsutism, weight, hair loss)

### Why?
- Hormonal imbalance (androgens, insulin) affects mood regulation
- Visible symptoms (acne, hair, weight) affect self-esteem
- Fertility concerns create emotional stress
- Chronic condition fatigue — the constant management burden

## Seeking Support

### Professional Help
- **Therapist/counselor**: CBT has strong evidence in PCOS for improving general health, mood, and disordered eating
- **Support groups**: Online and in-person PCOS communities reduce isolation
- **Peer support**: Talking with others who "get it" is therapeutic

### Self-Care Practices
- Set realistic goals — progress, not perfection
- Practice self-compassion regarding your appearance and health journey
- Connect with loved ones about what you're going through
- Schedule "non-negotiable" time for yourself weekly

## Body Image

- PCOS symptoms like hirsutism, acne, and weight gain can deeply affect body image
- Remind yourself: **PCOS is a medical condition, not a personal failure**
- If hirsutism causes distress, discuss treatments: laser hair removal, and anti-androgen medication with your doctor
- Acne treatment: dermatologist + spironolactone often help significantly
- Challenge media-led ideals — health looks different for everyone

## Sleep

Sleep deprivation worsens insulin resistance and mood. Target **7-9 hours** nightly.

### Sleep Hygiene
- Same wake time daily, even weekends
- No screens 1 hour before bed
- Cool, dark room
- Avoid caffeine after 2pm
- Evening wind-down routine (reading, stretching, warm shower)

## Summary

Exercise (aerobic + resistance), stress management, adequate sleep, and mental health support form the non-medication half of PCOS care. They compound with dietary and medical treatment. Be kind to yourself — you are managing a complex condition.
