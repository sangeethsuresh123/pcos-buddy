# PCOS Patient Assistance Guide

## What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a hormonal disorder common among women of reproductive age. It affects the ovaries and may cause irregular menstrual cycles, elevated androgen levels, and polycystic ovaries.

### Key Symptoms
- **Irregular periods**: Fewer than 8 periods per year, long gaps between periods, or absent periods
- **Excess androgens**: Elevated male hormones, causing physical signs such as excess facial/body hair (hirsutism), adult acne, or male-pattern baldness
- **Polycystic ovaries**: Enlarged ovaries with many small follicles (eggs fail to regularly release)

### Common Signs to Watch For
- Weight gain, especially around the abdomen
- Difficulty losing weight
- Darkening of skin along neck creases, groin, and underneath breasts (acanthosis nigricans)
- Skin tags in armpits or neck area
- Mood changes, depression, and anxiety

## Diagnosis Criteria (Rotterdam Criteria)

PCOS is diagnosed when at least **two** of the following three are present:
1. Irregular or absent ovulation
2. Clinical and/or biochemical signs of hyperandrogenism
3. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound

Other conditions must be excluded (thyroid disease, hyperprolactinemia, nonclassical congenital adrenal hyperplasia).

## Treatment and Management

### Lifestyle Modifications (First-Line Treatment)
- **Lifestyle** is the first-line treatment. Even a **5-10% reduction in body weight** can significantly restore ovulation and improve insulin resistance.
- **Exercise**: 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly plus resistance training 2-3 times per week.
- **Sleep**: 7-9 hours per night. Poor sleep worsens insulin resistance.

### Diet Recommendations
- **Anti-inflammatory, low glycemic index (GI) diet**
- Include: leafy greens, whole grains, lean protein (chicken, fish, tofu), legumes, nuts, seeds
- Reduce: refined carbohydrates (white bread, white rice, sugary drinks), processed foods, trans fats
- **Fiber**: 25-35g/day to manage glucose levels
- **Omega-3 fatty acids**: fatty fish, flaxseed, walnuts

### Common Supplements
Several supplements have shown benefit, often used alongside metformin:
- **Inositol (myo + D-chiro 40:1 ratio)**: improves insulin sensitivity and ovulation (~2000-4000mg daily)
- **Vitamin D**: many PCOS patients are deficient; correct levels through supplementation
- **Omega-3 fatty acids**: reduce inflammation and triglycerides
- **Chromium & Magnesium**: may support glucose metabolism

Always confirm supplement dosage with a healthcare professional.

### Medications
- **Metformin**: improves insulin sensitivity, may help with weight and ovulation. Start low and titrate slowly to minimize GI side effects.
- **Spironolactone**: an anti-androgen for hirsutism and acne (NOT suitable when pregnant or planning pregnancy).
- **Combined oral contraceptives (COCs)**: regulate cycles, reduce androgen levels, protect the endometrium.
- **Letrozole**: first-line ovulation induction for women trying to conceive.
- **Clomiphene citrate**: alternative ovulation induction agent.

## Fertility and Conception

- PCOS is the **most common cause of anovulatory infertility**.
- Weight loss of 5-10% and lifestyle changes can restore ovulation in many women.
- **Letrozole** is the first-line pharmacological option for ovulation induction.
- A combination approach (lifestyle + medication) yields higher live birth rates than either alone.
- For those who do not respond, in vitro fertilization (IVF) remains a successful option.

## Mental Health

- Women with PCOS have **3x higher odds of anxiety** and higher rates of depression.
- Body image concerns, fertility stress, and acne/hirsutism all contribute.
- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) has shown benefit in improving irregular eating, general health, and mood in PCOS.
- Don't hesitate to seek professional mental health support.

## Long-Term Health Risks

- **Type 2 diabetes**: up to 50% of women with PCOS develop diabetes by age 40 (vs 12% without)
- **Cardiovascular disease**: higher rates of dyslipidemia, hypertension, and metabolic syndrome
- **Endometrial hyperplasia/cancer**: from prolonged unopposed estrogen (lack of regular periods)
- **Sleep apnea**: more common in overweight PCOS patients
- **NAFLD**: non-alcoholic fatty liver disease

Annual screening: fasting glucose (or 2-hour OGTT), lipid panel, blood pressure, weight/BMI.

## Emergency / When to See a Doctor Immediately

- Pelvic pain that is severe or sudden
- Very heavy or prolonged bleeding (soaking through a pad/hour)
- Symptoms of blood clots or unusual swelling in legs
- Shortness of breath or chest pain
- Signs of diabetes complications (extreme thirst, frequent urination, blurred vision)

## Summary

PCOS is a common, treatable condition. A combination of lifestyle changes, medication, and mental health support produces the best outcomes. Every person's PCOS is unique — work with your healthcare team to personalize a plan.
